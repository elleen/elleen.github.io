# On "failure"
*November 22, 2026*

I might seem a bit negative the way I've written a couple of these less-than-ideal situations from my past. I promise I'm not! I'm so grateful for the good and the bad, and how they have shaped me and my current (lovely) life.

When I was in high school unsure what to pursue as a career; in college with endless SWE internship interviews; at my first job wondering if all the future held was *more* tech company; or in my mid-20s pushing myself to fulfill my prior, loftier goals — it would be deflating to only ever see others' seemingly perfect paths.

When I was in the thick of a grad school stint that wasn't going anywhere, one of the most helpful resources for me reading the blog of someone who was in the same boat as me. We were both once starry-eyed undergrads with rewarding research projects resulting in some shiny papers, but actual graduate school research didn't live up to what we'd thought it might look like[^1]. Knowing that someone who I'd felt so aligned with have the same reservations I was going through was the push I needed to get out of it. (Which, ultimately, felt like I would be failing myself, my undergrad advisors, my graduate school advisor, the fellowship program I was a part of, my friends, my partner...)

All's to say — I hope I'm being a bit more frank in the representation of my life and career trajectory, and that it's been confusing and challenging, but still fun. If you're in a similarly shaped boat as past me, and want to chat, my email is always open. 

[^1]: Coincidentally, I found out he *also* worked at Cloudflare, so when I started there I reached out. We had a lovely chat, and validated that leaving the PhD (in my situation) was a good decision.
