# On "failure"

I might seem like a negative Nancy the way I've written a couple of these less-than-ideal situations from my past experiences. I promise I don't intend to be! Life is life, and I'm grateful for the good and the bad and how it has shaped me. 

When I was younger, whether it was in college; at my first job wondering if all the future held was *more* software engineering; or in my mid-20s thinking I had to fulfill the dreams of when I was in college, it was tough to see the seemingly perfect paths of people I respected and looked up to. 

When I was in the thick of a grad school stint that wasn't going anywhere, one of the most helpful resources for me reading the blog of someone who was in the same boat as me. We were both once starry-eyed undergrads with rewarding research projects resulting in some shiny papers, but actual graduate school research not living up to what we'd thought it'd look like[^1]. Knowing that someone who I'd felt so aligned with have the same reservations I was going through was the push I needed to get out of it. I'd had the sunk cost fallacy of the couple years I'd sunk into doing nothing with my graduate school opportunity (which ultimately felt like I would be failing myself, my undergrad advisors, the fellowship program I was a part of, my friends, my partner...)

All's to say — I hope that me being a bit more frank is a small way of showing whoever might have stumbled on my website that it's not been all sunshine and rainbows. It's likely that not a soul is reading this, but if you are, and you want to chat, my email is always open. 

[^1]: Coincidentally, I found out he *also* worked at Cloudflare, so when I started there I reached out and we had a lovely chat. He was probably the first and only person to explicitly validate that leaving the PhD (in my situation) was a good decision.
