# On "failure"
*November 22, 2026*

I might seem like a negative Nancy the way I've written a couple of these less-than-ideal situations from my past experiences. I promise I don't intend to be! Life is life, and I'm grateful for the good and the bad and how it has shaped me and my current (lovely) life! 

When I was younger, in high school unsure what to pursue as a career; in college with endless SWE internship interviews; at my first job wondering if all the future held was *more* tech company; or in my mid-20s pushing myself to fulfill my prior, loftier goals — it would be deflating to only ever see others' seemingly perfect paths.

When I was in the thick of a grad school stint that wasn't going anywhere, one of the most helpful resources for me reading the blog of someone who was in the same boat as me. We were both once starry-eyed undergrads with rewarding research projects resulting in some shiny papers, but actual graduate school research didn't live up to what we'd thought it might look like[^1]. Knowing that someone who I'd felt so aligned with have the same reservations I was going through was the push I needed to get out of it. (Which, ultimately, felt like I would be failing myself, my undergrad advisors, my graduate school advisor, the fellowship program I was a part of, my friends, my partner...)

All's to say — I hope that I'm being a bit more frank in the representation of my past, and that it's not been all sunshine and rainbows. If you're in a similarly shaped boat as past me, and want to chat, my email is always open. 

[^1]: Coincidentally, I found out he *also* worked at Cloudflare, so when I started there I reached out and we had a lovely chat. He was probably the first and only person to explicitly validate that leaving the PhD (in my situation) was a good decision.
